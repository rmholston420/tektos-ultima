# Financial Services Regulatory Compliance Plan

**Version:** 1.0  
**Effective Date:** 2026-08-21  
**Owner:** Chief Compliance Officer  
**Review Cycle:** Annual

---

## 1. Regulatory Landscape Mapping

| Regulation | Scope | Key Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **Basel III** | Capital adequacy, leverage ratios, liquidity coverage | Maintain minimum capital ratios; conduct stress tests; publish quarterly disclosures |
| **Dodd-Frank** | Systemic risk, consumer protection, derivatives | Volcker Rule compliance; CFTC/SEC reporting; stress testing for large institutions |
| **MiFID II** | EU markets, transparency, investor protection | Best execution reporting; product governance; transaction reporting to competent authorities |
| **PSD2** | EU payment services, open banking | Strong customer authentication (SCA); API security; third-party provider oversight |
| **AML/KYC** | Anti-money laundering, know-your-customer | Customer due diligence (CDD); suspicious activity reporting (SAR); beneficial ownership verification |

**Action:** Maintain a living regulatory register updated quarterly by the Compliance team.

## 2. Compliance Risk Assessment

### 2.1 Regulatory Risk
- Identify applicable regulations by business line and jurisdiction
- Assess regulatory change impact using a heat map (likelihood × impact)
- Prioritize remediation efforts based on risk scoring

### 2.2 Operational Risk
- Map key processes to associated compliance controls
- Conduct annual process risk assessments
- Track and remediate control deficiencies within defined SLAs

### 2.3 Model Risk
- Implement model inventory and validation framework
- Require independent validation for all material models
- Monitor model performance drift on a quarterly basis

**Risk Rating Scale:** Critical → High → Medium → Low

## 3. Governance Framework

### Board Oversight
- Board of Directors receives quarterly compliance reports
- Annual compliance risk appetite statement approved by the Board
- Critical breaches reported to the Board within 24 hours

### Compliance Committee
- Chaired by the Chief Compliance Officer
- Meets monthly; includes heads of Risk, Legal, Operations, and IT
- Decision authority on policy exceptions and remediation priorities

### Three Lines of Defense

| Line | Function | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **First Line** | Business units | Own and manage compliance risks day-to-day |
| **Second Line** | Compliance, Risk Management | Set standards, monitor, challenge, and report |
| **Third Line** | Internal Audit | Independent assurance on governance and controls |

## 4. Policy and Procedure Development

### Policy Lifecycle
1. **Drafting** – Compliance owner drafts based on regulatory requirements
2. **Review** – Legal and Risk review for consistency and completeness
3. **Approval** – Required sign-off: CCO (all policies), CEO (critical policies)
4. **Publication** – Deploy to centralized policy management system
5. **Acknowledgment** – Staff must acknowledge within 10 business days
6. **Review** – Annual review cycle; triggered updates for regulatory changes

### Version Control & Approval Workflows
- All policies tracked in a centralized repository with version history
- Change log documents what changed, why, and by whom
- Approval workflows enforced through the policy management system
- Emergency policy changes require expedited CCO + CEO approval

---

## 5. Monitoring and Reporting

### Regulatory Reporting
- Calendar of all regulatory filing deadlines maintained centrally
- Automated submission where possible; manual submissions double-checked
- Error resolution within 48 hours of detection

### Exception Reporting
- Real-time alerts for control breaches and threshold violations
- Escalation matrix defined for each exception severity level
- Root cause analysis required for all Material incidents

### Trend Analysis
- Quarterly compliance metrics dashboard for management
- Year-over-year comparison of key indicators
- Proactive identification of emerging risk patterns

---

## 6. Audit Management

### Internal Audit
- Risk-based audit plan approved annually by the Audit Committee
- Coverage of all material business lines and critical functions every 3 years
- Management action plans due within 30 days of audit finding

### External Audit
- Coordinate external auditor access and information requests
- Track and remediate external audit findings per agreed timelines
- Annual review of auditor independence and qualifications

### Regulatory Examination Readiness
- Maintain an "always ready" file: policies, records, training logs
- Pre-examination mock reviews conducted quarterly
- Designated liaison officer for all regulatory interactions
- Post-examination remediation tracking with weekly status updates

## 9. Incident Response and Breach Notification

### Classification
| Severity | Criteria | Response Time |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Regulatory breach, material loss, data breach affecting >1,000 individuals | Immediate |
| High | Significant control failure, potential regulatory action | 4 hours |
| Medium | Process deviation, contained issue | 24 hours |
| Low | Minor procedural gap, no regulatory exposure | 5 business days |

### Regulatory Timelines
- **GDPR-style breaches:** Notify regulator within 72 hours; affected individuals without undue delay
- **PSD2/SCA incidents:** Notify competent authority within 24 hours
- **AML/SAR-related incidents:** File SAR within regulatory timeframe (varies by jurisdiction); no tipping-off
- **Basel III capital breaches:** Notify supervisor immediately

### Communication Plan
- Internal: Incident Commander notifies CCO, CEO, and Board Chair within 1 hour (Critical)
- External: Legal and Compliance coordinate with regulator; PR handles public communications
- Post-incident: Root cause analysis within 10 business days; remediation plan within 20 business days

## 10. Cross-Border Compliance

### Jurisdictional Mapping
- Maintain a matrix of applicable regulations by jurisdiction and business activity
- Identify home and host regulator requirements for each operating entity
- Quarterly review for new or changing jurisdictional obligations

### Data Transfer Restrictions
- EU → non-EU transfers require Standard Contractual Clauses or adequacy decision
- Implement data localization controls where required (e.g., China PIPL, Russia data laws)
- Regular assessment of transfer mechanisms and legal basis
- Privacy Impact Assessments required for new cross-border data flows

### Key Cross-Border Considerations
| Region | Key Restriction | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| EU/EEA | GDPR data export rules | SCCs + Transfer Impact Assessments |
| UK | UK GDPR post-Brexit | Separate UK data processing register |
| China | PIPL cross-border transfer rules | Local data storage + security assessment |
| US | State-level privacy laws (CCPA, etc.) | Jurisdiction-specific privacy controls |
